# Pillar 1: Mine Planning & Scheduling

---

## Overview

Mine Planning & Scheduling is the IT-intensive, model-driven domain that converts geological and economic inputs into actionable production plans at every time horizon — from a Life-of-Mine schedule spanning decades to a shift dispatch instruction valid for the next twelve hours. It is the sole pillar operating predominantly at ISA-95 Level 4 (enterprise planning and logistics) and Level 3 (manufacturing operations management), with minimal Level 0–2 activity. The pillar does not directly generate physical data; instead, it generates the plan data that is consumed and reconciled against actuals from Pillars 2 and 3.

The four L2 process areas reflect the planning time hierarchy: Strategic & Long-Term Planning (LOM) establishes the mine's envelope, resource utilisation strategy, and economic viability over the full life of the asset. Medium-Term Planning (quarterly/monthly) translates the LOM schedule into executable phase sequences with equipment fleet commitments and blend targets. Short-Term Planning (weekly/daily) produces the dig polygon designs and production target setting that directly drive shift-level operations. Shift Execution & Dispatch closes the loop — generating the shift roster, making operator-equipment assignments, optimising FMS dispatch, and tracking production actuals in real time.

The critical dependency that distinguishes this pillar from the others: it is both the origin of planning instructions consumed by Pillars 2–4, and the reconciliation endpoint that consumes actuals from Pillars 2 and 3. The block model is the central shared entity — it is the spatial-geological foundation from which all planning decisions derive, and it is updated through a feedback loop from mine production actuals (grade control reconciliation). This feedback mechanism is the ontological backbone of mine-to-mill reconciliation and is where the DDVC architecture must enable closed-loop data flow.

Commodity distinction (copper vs. zinc) creates minimal structural differences in the planning process model. Differences appear in cut-off grade parameters, blend strategy (Cu grade vs. Zn/Pb grade and ratio), and deposit geometry (porphyry Cu — relatively simple spatial grade distribution vs. sedex Zn at Red Dog — complex stratigraphy and limited spatial extent driving tighter sequencing constraints). These are flagged at the relevant activities.

## Process Decomposition

> **Column guide**: This pillar operates almost entirely at ISA-95 Levels 3–4. Where Level 2 interface points exist (FMS dispatch integration), they are explicitly noted. DDVC Layer relevance: planning artefacts feed primarily the Curated layer as master/reference data (block model, bench, polygon) and the Contextualized layer as plan-vs-actual analysis. Raw layer participation is minimal — it is confined to FMS actuals ingestion.

## Process Flow Narratives (BPMN-Ready)

### 1.1 Strategic & Long-Term Planning — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Strategic Planning | **Lanes**: Database Geologist, Resource Geologist / Geostatistician, Mine Planning Engineer, Chief Mine Planner, Competent Person

**[Trigger: Annual planning cycle or material resource/price update]**

1. **Compile and Validate Drill Hole Data** (Database Geologist) — all new drill hole data compiled, QA/QC checked, exceptions quarantined.
2. **Construct / Update Geological Wireframes** (Resource Geologist) — lithological and mineralisation domain wireframes updated in geological modeling software.
3. **Validate Geological Model** (Resource Geologist + Competent Person) — model validated against drill data; sign-off required.
   - *[GATEWAY — Model passes validation?]* → Yes: proceed. No: revise interpretation, re-validate.
4. **Model Variography** (Geostatistician) — variogram fitting per domain per metal.
5. **Interpolate Grade into Block Model** (Geostatistician) — kriging run; grade and density assigned per block per domain.
6. **Validate and Classify Resource** (Geostatistician + Competent Person) — resource classification applied; resource statement prepared.
   - *[GATEWAY — CP satisfied?]* → Yes: block model released. No: revise interpolation parameters.
7. **Define Pit Optimisation Parameters** (Chief Mine Planner) — price, cost, recovery, slope angles set; parameter set version-controlled.
8. **Execute Pit Shell Optimisation** (Mine Planning Engineer) — nested shells generated at 0.5–1.5 revenue factors.
9. **Select Optimal Pushback Strategy** (Chief Mine Planner) — pushback sequence selected from nested shell analysis; NPV compared across alternatives.
10. **Design Mining Phases and Bench Geometry** (Mine Planning Engineer) — phase and bench 3D geometry drawn in mine planning software.
11. **Generate Annual Production Schedule** (Mine Planning Engineer) — year-by-year schedule generated in scheduling software; LOM record produced.
12. **Calculate Break-Even Cut-Off Grade** (Chief Mine Planner + Chief Metallurgist) — COG calculated per commodity; sensitivity analysis prepared.
13. **Review and Approve Cut-Off Grade** (Chief Mine Planner + VP Operations + CFO) — formal sign-off; COG version-controlled and distributed.
    - *[GATEWAY — Material COG change (>10%)?]* → Yes: CFO sign-off required; notify grade control. No: standard sign-off.

**End Event**: LOM schedule, block model version, ultimate pit shell, and approved COG released. Medium-term planning cycle commences.

### 1.2 Medium-Term Planning — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Medium-Term Planning | **Lanes**: Mine Planning Engineer, Mine Superintendent, Geotechnical Engineer, Chief Metallurgist

**[Trigger: Monthly planning cycle — typically 5–10 working days before period start]**

1. **Survey Current Mine Position** (Survey Team) — drone or GPS survey; as-mined DTM imported into mine planning software.
2. **Sequence Bench Faces for Period** (Mine Planning Engineer) — bench face sequence selected for 3-month window from as-mined starting position.
3. **Review Slope Monitoring Data** (Geotechnical Engineer) — displacement and piezometer data reviewed against trigger levels.
   - *[GATEWAY — Geotechnical trigger reached?]* → Yes: constraint applied to plan (no-mining zone or face suspension). No: proceed.
4. **Apply Geotechnical Constraints** (Mine Planning Engineer + Geotechnical Engineer) — constraints applied as hard limits in scheduling software.
5. **Model Equipment Productivity** (Mine Planning Engineer) — TUM-based productivity calculated; fleet requirement by period confirmed.
6. **Allocate Fleet to Mining Areas** (Mine Superintendent + Mine Planning Engineer) — equipment assigned to phases/areas.
7. **Forecast Mill Feed Grade** (Mine Planning Engineer) — block model queried for planned sequence; head grade predicted by metal.
8. **Optimise Blend Using Stockpile** (Mine Planning Engineer + Chief Metallurgist) — stockpile drawdown/build instruction generated to achieve blend target.
   - *[GATEWAY — Blend target achievable from direct mine + stockpile?]* → Yes: plan issued. No: sequence adjustment required or target relaxed with metallurgist approval.

**End Event**: 3-month rolling plan issued; fleet allocation confirmed; blend targets and stockpile instructions distributed.

### 1.3 Short-Term Planning — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Short-Term Planning | **Lanes**: Short-Term Mine Planner, Grade Control Geologist, Mine Superintendent, FMS System

**[Trigger: Weekly planning session; daily face advance]**

1. **Receive Monthly Plan** (Short-Term Mine Planner) — monthly bench sequence and daily target rate confirmed.
2. **Design Dig Polygon Boundaries** (Short-Term Mine Planner) — polygon drawn on current bench surface in mine planning software; volume and predicted grade calculated from block model.
3. **Approve Polygon** (Mine Superintendent) — geotechnical clearance confirmed; polygon signed off.
   - *[GATEWAY — Geotechnical constraint in polygon area?]* → Yes: geotechnical engineer provides clearance or constraint. No: standard approval.
4. **Release Polygon to Operations** (Short-Term Mine Planner) — polygon released to grade control (sampling plan trigger) and Drill & Blast (pattern brief trigger, 1.3.2).
5. **Extract Pattern Parameters and Issue Brief** (Short-Term Mine Planner → Drill & Blast Engineer) — pattern brief issued; triggers Pillar 2 (2.1.1) blast design.
6. **Set Daily Production Targets** (Mine Superintendent + Short-Term Mine Planner) — daily tonnes and grade targets set; entered in shift reporting system and FMS.
7. **Pre-Assign Material Destination** (Short-Term Mine Planner + Grade Control Geologist) — default destination uploaded to FMS per material type per polygon zone.
8. **Receive Grade Control Assay Results** (Grade Control Geologist — from LIMS, Pillar 2) — results compared to block model prediction.
   - *[GATEWAY — Material reclassification required?]* → Yes: FMS destination updated; reclassification_event logged; block model variance recorded. No: pre-blast assignment confirmed.

**End Event**: Approved polygon in FMS with confirmed destination assignment; blast design triggered; daily targets active.

### 1.4 Shift Execution & Dispatch — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Shift Execution | **Lanes**: Shift Supervisor / HR, Mine Controller, Operator, FMS Auto-dispatch

**[Trigger: Shift start — typically 60 min before shift commencement for pre-shift planning]**

1. **Confirm Operator Availability** (Shift Supervisor) — attendance confirmed; certifications checked; absentees replaced or production capacity adjusted.
2. **Assign Operators to Equipment** (Mine Controller) — operator-equipment-shift assignments made; entered in FMS.
3. **Log Operators onto FMS** (Operators) — each operator logs onto assigned equipment FMS terminal; operator-equipment-shift linkage active.
4. **Activate FMS Dispatch** (Mine Controller + FMS) — daily production targets and face assignments loaded; dispatch algorithm active.
5. **[PARALLEL LOOP — continuous during shift]**
   - **Monitor Real-Time Production** (Mine Controller) — running tonnes actuals vs. target reviewed every 15–30 min via FMS dashboard.
   - **Optimise Truck-Shovel Assignment** (FMS Auto-dispatch) — continuous real-time dispatch assignment per truck cycle completion.
   - *[GATEWAY — Production tracking more than 10% below target?]* → Yes: mine controller intervenes — face prioritisation change, dispatch adjustment, or escalation to Mine Superintendent. No: continue.
   - *[INTERMEDIATE EVENT — Equipment breakdown?]* → Mine controller records TUM: DT delay event; maintenance notified; FMS reassigns affected truck's cycles.
   - *[INTERMEDIATE EVENT — Grade control reclassification?]* → Mine controller updates destination in FMS; reclassification_event logged.
   - *[INTERMEDIATE EVENT — Destination full or unavailable?]* → Mine controller overrides FMS destination; logs override with reason code.
6. **Close Shift in FMS** (Mine Controller) — all open haul cycles confirmed; delay events TUM-classified and validated.
7. **Produce Shift Actuals Report** (Mine Controller + Shift Supervisor) — shift report generated: tonnes, grade estimate, utilisation, delay summary (TUM), variance from target.
8. **Reconcile Actuals Against Plan** (Mine Planning Engineer) — daily: shift actuals vs. daily target; weekly/monthly: cumulative vs. plan; grade variance (block model vs. grade control) logged as reclassification events.

**End Event**: Shift report filed; production actuals in shift reporting system; grade variance events available for block model update cycle.

## Pillar 1 — Mine Planning & Scheduling: Cross-Pillar Dependencies (Pillars 5 & 6)

### Pillar 5 (Maintenance & Reliability) Dependencies

| Pillar 1 Activity | Dependency Direction | Pillar 5 Entity / Activity | Integration Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2.2.1 Calculate Fleet Requirement | Pillar 1 consumes Pillar 5 | `equipment_criticality_record` (availability factor); historical MTBF/MTTR by equipment class (from `equipment_failure_record` aggregates) | Fleet requirement calculation uses availability factor = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR) per equipment class. Without actual reliability data from Pillar 5, this defaults to a static assumption — typically 85-90% for haul trucks, 75-80% for rope shovels. The MFD Contextualized product must expose these statistics so Pillar 1 planners use data-driven availability factors rather than static assumptions. |
| 1.2.2.2 Allocate Equipment to Mining Areas | Pillar 1 consumes Pillar 5 | `maintenance_schedule` (planned downtime windows) | The maintenance schedule must be loaded into mine planning software as a constraint before fleet allocation is finalised. Units with scheduled maintenance windows within the planning period are excluded from the available fleet for those windows. This is currently often informal — a data product integration between the EAM maintenance schedule and the mine planning tool would formalise it. |
| 1.4.2.1 Conduct Equipment Pre-Use Check | Pillar 1/2 generates Pillar 5 | `work_order` (P1 corrective) | Pre-use check failures generate corrective work orders in Pillar 5 (SAP PM). The failed equipment is immediately removed from the available fleet (FMS state change to Maintenance) and a P1 work order is raised. The pre-use check failure record must carry the equipment ID, the defect description, and a timestamp — these are the opening entries of the corrective work order. |
| 1.4.4.3 Generate and Commit Shift Production Report | Pillar 1 generates Pillar 5 input | `shift_production_report` provides TUM: DT duration for reliability analysis | The committed shift production report contains TUM: DT durations by equipment unit by shift. These are the denominator inputs for MTBF calculation (MTBF = total operating hours / number of failure events). Without the committed shift report feeding the reliability system, operating hours are unmeasured. |
