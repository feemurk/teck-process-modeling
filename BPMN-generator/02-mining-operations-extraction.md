# Pillar 2: Mining Operations (Extraction)

---

## Overview

Mining Operations (Extraction) encompasses the physical extraction of material from an open-pit mine, from the moment a blast pattern is designed through to fragmented material delivered to its designated destination — primary crusher, ROM stockpile, or waste dump. The pillar comprises three process areas: Drill & Blast, Load & Haul, and Mine Services, each generating distinct but tightly coupled data streams that must ultimately reconcile against the mine plan.

This pillar is the highest-velocity data-generation domain in the open-pit value chain. The majority of its activity operates at ISA-95 Levels 0–2 (physical process, sensors, and control/monitoring systems), with Level 3 functions present in dispatch, shift reporting, and delay classification. ISA-95 Level 4 is a consumer of this pillar's outputs (reconciliation against plan) rather than a generator within it. Every delay event, equipment state, and time-based KPI must conform to the GMG Time Usage Model (TUM) taxonomy — this is non-negotiable and is the ontological backbone of the Mobile Fleet Data (MFD) curated data product.

The commodity distinction (copper vs. zinc) has minimal structural impact on this pillar. Processing routes diverge downstream; at the extraction layer, differences are confined to cut-off grade thresholds applied to material classification, deposit geometry (porphyry vs. sedex affects bench geometry and muck pile characteristics), and Arctic logistics constraints at Red Dog (seasonal blasting windows, freeze-thaw effects on blast design, haul road conditions). These are flagged at the relevant activities but do not alter the process model structure.

## Process Flow Narratives (BPMN-Ready)

### 2.1 Drill & Blast

**Pool**: Drill & Blast | **Lanes**: Mine Planning, Drill & Blast Engineer, Drill Rig Operator, Shot-firer, Grade Control Geologist, FMS/System

**Start Event**: Short-term plan approved; dig polygon released.

1. **Receive Dig Polygon** (Engineer) — receives approved polygon and bench design from mine planning.
2. **Design Blast Pattern** (Engineer) — calculates burden, spacing, depth, powder factor; selects explosive product; produces blast pattern design file.
3. **Survey and Stake Pattern** (Survey Tech + FMS) — stakes collar positions; uploads surveyed coordinates to FMS and blast design software.
4. **Assign Drill Rig to Pattern** (FMS/Mine Controller) — available, authorised rig dispatched; collar waypoints loaded to drill guidance system.
5. **Tram Drill Rig to First Hole** (Drill Rig Operator) — **TUM: NP** during transit.
6. **Execute Drilling with MWD Capture** (Drill Rig Operator + Drill Guidance System) — drill to designed depth; MWD captured at 1-sec; **TUM: PT** during active drilling.
   - *[LOOP]* — repeats per hole in pattern; FMS sequences hole assignment.
7. **Measure Hole Depth and Water** (Drill Rig Operator) — as-drilled depth and water depth recorded; wet/dry flag set.
8. **QC Drill Holes** (Drill & Blast Engineer) — as-drilled vs. design.
   - *[GATEWAY — All holes pass QC?]* → Yes: proceed to charging. No: issue remediation (re-drill or redesign affected holes).
9. **Dispatch Explosive Truck to Pattern** (FMS/Mine Controller) — **TUM for explosive truck: NP** transit.
10. **Load Explosive per Hole** (Shot-firer + Explosive Truck Operator) — pump/pour per design; mass per hole recorded; EBS detonator programmed per hole (if electronic system).
11. **Stem Holes** (Shot-firer) — apply stemming to specified depth.
12. **Install Initiation Network** (Shot-firer) — surface network connected; as-built delay sequence recorded.
13. **Establish Exclusion Zone and Clear** (Supervisor + Mine Controller) — all equipment evacuated; mine controller records **delay_event** for equipment within zone (**TUM: OD** or **TUM: SBO** per classification authority's determination).
14. **Initiate Blast** (Shot-firer) — blast fired; initiation timestamp (UTC) recorded; EBS log archived.
15. **Conduct Post-Blast Safety Inspection** (Shot-firer + Supervisor) — walk inspection after re-entry wait; misfire check.
    - *[GATEWAY — Misfire detected?]* → Yes: activate misfire protocol. No: issue safety clearance.
16. **Assess Fragmentation** (Engineer) — photogrammetric analysis; P80 computed vs. design target.
    - *[GATEWAY — P80 within specification?]* → Yes: proceed. No: issue re-blast assessment.
17. **Collect Grade Control Samples** (Grade Control Geologist) — chip/channel samples per sampling grid.
18. **Submit to Laboratory** (Grade Control Geologist + LIMS) — samples logged in LIMS; await assay TAT (8–24 hours).
19. **Classify Material and Assign Destination** (Grade Control Geologist + FMS) — assay results applied to cut-off grade; per-zone classification and destination uploaded to FMS.

**End Event**: Material classification and destination assignment active in FMS; muck pile ready for Load & Haul.

### 2.2 Load & Haul

**Pool**: Load & Haul | **Lanes**: Mine Controller / FMS, Shovel Operator, Dozer Operator, Truck Operator

**Start Event**: Shift start — roster active; dig face assignment from FMS; muck pile classified and available.

**[PARALLEL FORK]** — Multiple haul trucks and shovel(s) operate concurrently. The following describes one truck's repeating haul cycle. Multiple instances execute in parallel across the active fleet.

1. **Assign Equipment to Shift** (Mine Controller + FMS) — operator-equipment assignments loaded; shift plan active.
2. **Tram Shovel to Dig Face** (Shovel Operator + FMS) — **TUM: NP** during tram.
3. **Clean and Dress Dig Face** (Dozer Operator) — grades approach ramp and floor; **TUM: WT** for dozer.
4. **Dispatch Truck to Face** (FMS Auto-dispatch or Mine Controller) — truck receives face assignment on cab display; en route.
5. **Queue Truck at Shovel** (Truck Operator) — arrives at face; enters queue if shovel occupied.
   - *[GATEWAY — Queue duration vs. OD threshold?]* → Below threshold: **TUM: OD**. Threshold exceeded + management decision: **TUM: SBO** — mine controller must explicitly classify.
6. **Spot Truck at Shovel** (Truck Operator + Shovel Operator) — manoeuvres to loading position; **TUM: NP**.
7. **Load Truck** (Shovel Operator) — successive bucket passes; strut pressure telemetry confirms accumulation; **TUM: PT** for shovel, **TUM: WT** for truck.
8. **Verify Payload and Issue Dispatch** (FMS) — payload estimated from strut pressures; destination issued; haul_cycle record initiated.
   - *[GATEWAY — Payload within tolerance?]* → Overloaded: shovel operator removes partial pass (or continues with flag). Underloaded: FMS flags for review.
9. **Execute Loaded Haul** (Truck Operator + FMS tracks) — truck travels to destination; **TUM: WT**.
   - *[INTERMEDIATE EVENT — Breakdown during haul?]* → Truck stops; mine controller classifies **TUM: DT**; haul_cycle suspended.
10. **Queue at Destination** (Truck Operator) — arrives; queues if access blocked.
    - *[GATEWAY — Queue classification?]* → **TUM: OD** (inherent brief wait) or **TUM: SBO** (management decision — crusher shutdown, access congestion).
11. **Spot Truck at Dump** (Truck Operator) — manoeuvre to dump position; safety berm confirmed; **TUM: NP**.
12. **Dump Load** (Truck Operator) — hydraulic dump; strut pressure drop confirms empty; dump event timestamp and haul_cycle dump record written to FMS; **TUM: WT** (dumping is productive function).
13. **Execute Empty Return** (Truck Operator + FMS) — truck body lowered; new dispatch assignment received; travels empty; **TUM: NP**.
    - *[INTERMEDIATE EVENT — Operational delay en route?]* → Fuelling, operator change, short wait → **TUM: OD**.

**[LOOP BACK]** — Empty return completes one haul_cycle record. Loop repeats until: shift end; fuel/service interval; face exhausted; equipment breakdown.

**[PARALLEL JOIN]** — At shift end, mine controller reconciles all open haul_cycle records; shift-level production committed to FMS.

**End Event**: Shift end — all open haul_cycle records closed; shift production total committed to FMS database.

### 2.3 Mine Services

**Pool**: Mine Services | **Lanes**: Services Supervisor, Road Maintenance Operator, Dewatering Operator / SCADA, Geotechnical Engineer

**[PARALLEL — Three service streams operate continuously and independently during the shift]**

**Haul Road Maintenance Stream**

**Start Event**: Shift start or supervisor inspection identifies road requiring attention.

1. **Assess Road Condition** (Services Supervisor) — visual inspection or complaint from haul truck operator; segments prioritised.
2. **Assign Grader and Water Truck** (Services Supervisor + FMS) — dispatched to road segments.
3. **Grade Road Segment** (Road Maintenance Operator) — systematic grading pass; **TUM: WT** for grader; segment, duration, and operator recorded.
4. **Apply Dust Suppression** (Road Maintenance Operator) — water applied; **TUM: WT** for water truck; volume applied recorded.
   - *[LOOP]* — repeats across all assigned segments per shift.

**End Event**: Shift complete; road maintenance records submitted.

**Pit Dewatering Stream**

**Start Event**: Continuous — pump SCADA monitors sump level autonomously.

1. **Monitor Sump Level** (Dewatering SCADA) — automated level sensor; high setpoint triggers pump start.
2. **Start Pump** (SCADA auto / Operator manual override) — pump starts; telemetry stream begins to PI Historian.
3. **Monitor Pump Operation** (Dewatering Operator) — periodic review of flow, pressure, current.
   - *[GATEWAY — Pump fault alarm?]* → Fault: operator responds; maintenance notified; pump offline. Normal: continue.
4. **Stop Pump** (SCADA auto) — sump falls below low setpoint; run time recorded.

**End Event**: Continuous — no discrete shift-end event; operational records summarised per shift.

**Slope Stability Monitoring Stream**

**Start Event**: Continuous — instruments report on automated scan schedule (15–60 min).

1. **Collect Displacement Data** (Automated — Total Station / GBSAR) — scheduled scan; displacement vectors computed; archived.
2. **Review Displacement Data** (Geotechnical Engineer) — latest readings vs. alert threshold.
   - *[GATEWAY — Alert threshold exceeded?]* → Normal: log reading, continue. Alert: proceed to assessment.
3. **Assess Stability Event** (Geotechnical Engineer) — review trend data; determine cause and risk level.
   - *[GATEWAY — Operational constraint required?]* → No constraint: document, continue monitoring. Constraint required: issue exclusion zone or access restriction.
4. **Issue Operational Constraint** (Geotechnical Engineer + Mine Controller) — mine controller enforces exclusion zone; affected equipment records **TUM: OD** or **TUM: SBO** per duration and nature of restriction.

**End Event**: Monitoring is continuous; alerts resolved or escalated per geotechnical protocol.

## Commodity Variations

| Process Area | Variation Type | Copper (QB, HVC, Antamina, CdA) | Zinc (Red Dog) | Data Implication |
|--------------|---------------|----------------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **2.1.1 Blast Design** | Rock characteristics | Porphyry Cu — typically medium to hard rock (UCS 80–180 MPa); large, relatively homogeneous blast patterns; SAG/ball mill circuit imposes P80 target of 150–250 mm for primary crusher feed | Sedex/carbonate-hosted Zn — highly variable hardness; ore/waste geotechnical contrast can be significant; bench dimensions constrained by deposit geometry | `blast_pattern` requires `rock_type_classification` attribute (porphyry / sedex / carbonate); P80 target attributes carry different ranges |
| **2.1.1.3 Explosive Selection** | Explosive type and constraints | Standard powder factors 0.3–0.6 kg/t; ANFO predominantly in dry holes | Higher powder factors may be required for competent carbonate host; Arctic temperatures (−40°C) constrain emulsion product performance — cold-temperature formulations required | `explosive_product` reference entity must include temperature performance rating; blast design software must carry temperature constraint rules |
| **2.1.5.2 Material Classification** | Cut-off grade | Cu cut-off grade (e.g., 0.15–0.20% Cu at HVC; higher at QB); molybdenum by-product classification at HVC/QB/Antamina | Zn cut-off grade (3–5% Zn at Red Dog); Pb by-product classification | `material_classification` requires separate grade attributes per commodity (`cu_pct`, `zn_pct`, `pb_pct`, `mo_pct`); cut-off grade is a parameterised reference, not hardcoded |
| **2.2.2.3 Shovel Loading** | Equipment type | Rope shovels (P&H, Komatsu) dominant at QB/HVC (large-scale, high-capacity pits); hydraulic excavators for secondary faces | Hydraulic excavators more prevalent at Red Dog — deposit geometry and bench scale less suited to large rope shovels | `equipment_class` distinguishes rope_shovel vs. hydraulic_excavator; payload estimation approach may differ (dipper vs. bucket volume) |
| **2.2.3.1 Loaded Haul** | Haul road conditions | Year-round operation; seasonal freeze-thaw variation; large-format haul trucks (220–360t class) | Arctic seasonal variation severe — gravel pad roads over permafrost; smaller-format trucks on some roads; seasonal haul road closures produce **TUM: SBE** (External Standby — not required) | `delay_event` reason code library must include Arctic-specific categories (permafrost road closure, freeze-thaw constraint, seasonal closure) |
| **2.3.1 Haul Road Maintenance** | Dust suppression and road type | Standard water truck dust suppression; calcium chloride treatment in dry summer | Arctic conditions limit dust suppression season; ice road maintenance procedures differ significantly | `service_event` requires season flag and maintenance type code accommodating arctic road types |
| **2.3.2 Pit Dewatering** | Dewatering scale and strategy | Active pit dewatering year-round; standard groundwater management for open-pit | Arctic permafrost — ice lensing and freeze-thaw complicate dewatering; winter pump operation risk (freezing rising mains); seasonal dewatering strategy fundamentally different | `dewatering_event` requires arctic_season flag; pump operating parameters differ for freeze-risk environments |

## Pillar 2 — Mining Operations: Cross-Pillar Dependencies (Pillars 5 & 6)

### Pillar 5 (Maintenance & Reliability) Dependencies

| Pillar 2 Entity / Activity | Dependency Direction | Pillar 5 Entity / Activity | Integration Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| `delay_event` (TUM: DT) | Pillar 2 generates Pillar 5 demand | `work_order` (corrective — triggered by breakdown) | **The most critical cross-pillar linkage in the programme.** Every TUM: DT delay event on mobile equipment should carry a `work_order_id` foreign key referencing the Pillar 5 corrective work order that addressed the failure. Without this link, TUM: DT analytics can identify that equipment was down but cannot explain why (failure mode, component, repair type). The data product contract for `delay_event` must mandate `work_order_id` as a required attribute for all DT events, not optional. |
| `mobile_equipment` (Pillar 2 master entity) | Shared entity | `work_order`, `equipment_failure_record`, `equipment_criticality_record` | The `mobile_equipment` entity (equipment_id, fleet_number, equipment_class, nominal_payload) is shared across Pillars 2 and 5. It must be a single curated master entity with Pillar 2 as the operational data owner (FMS) and Pillar 5 as the maintenance data owner (EAM/SAP PM). Collibra must designate one data steward and one source of truth for each attribute. |
| 2.1.2.1 Navigate Drill Rig to Hole | Pillar 2 generates Pillar 5 input | Operating hours counter in FMS → triggers PM work order | Drill rig operating hours accumulate from FMS dispatch records. Hours-based PM intervals (e.g., 250-hour service) are triggered when the FMS operating hours counter reaches the PM threshold. EAM must receive the FMS operating hours feed to generate PM work orders automatically — this system integration (FMS → SAP PM) is a critical enabler for predictive and preventive maintenance on mobile equipment. |
| 2.2.2.3 Load Truck with Shovel (strut pressure telemetry) | Pillar 2 generates Pillar 5 condition data | `condition_monitoring_telemetry` | Haul truck strut pressure telemetry (PI Historian) serves a dual purpose: payload estimation (Pillar 2) and suspension condition monitoring (Pillar 5). Abnormal strut pressure patterns (asymmetric loading, pressure loss, slow accumulation) are early indicators of strut seal failure or strut end damage. Pillar 5 condition monitoring must configure PI Historian alerts on strut pressure anomaly signatures, not just use strut data for payload. |

### Pillar 6 (Health & Safety) Dependencies

| Pillar 2 Entity / Activity | Dependency Direction | Pillar 6 Entity / Activity | Integration Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1.4.1 Establish and Clear Exclusion Zone | Pillar 6 governs | `risk_register_record` (explosives — blast exclusion); `field_hazard_assessment` (pre-blast) | The blast exclusion zone is a Pillar 6 safety control for the explosives hazard. Exclusion zone radius and clearance procedures must be specified in the Pillar 6 risk register for blasting activities. The all-clear confirmation before blast initiation constitutes a safety clearance that should be formally recorded — it is the safety authority equivalent of a permit cancellation. |
| 2.1.4.2 Initiate Blast | Pillar 6 governs | `permit_to_work` (blast authorisation — in some jurisdictions) | In some jurisdictions (MSHA), blast initiation requires formal authorisation from a certified blaster. The certification and authorisation may constitute a PTW-class document. Confirm with each site safety team whether blast initiation is covered by the PTW system or by an independent blasting authorisation document. |
| 2.2.2.1 Queue Truck at Shovel | Pillar 6 potential trigger | `incident_record` (near-miss: vehicle interaction) | Vehicle interactions — trucks colliding, near-miss during spotting, pedestrian proximity during truck spotting manoeuvres — are among the most common fatal hazard categories at open-pit mines (ICMM Fatal Hazard Control #1: Vehicle Interaction). FMS proximity alert data (if FMS has proximity detection configured) is a critical evidence source for vehicle interaction incident investigation. |
| 2.3.3 Slope Stability Monitoring | Pillar 6 governs | `risk_register_record` (geotechnical: slope failure); `incident_record` (if slope movement results in near-miss or injury) | Slope monitoring is a Pillar 6 risk control for the slope failure hazard. Displacement alerts from the monitoring system (Pillar 2, 2.3.3) are safety alerts governed by the Pillar 6 risk register and response protocol. The slope monitoring alert → exclusion zone → delay_event chain must include a Pillar 6 safety governance step: the geotechnical engineer's assessment and the safety officer's authorisation of the exclusion zone. |
| `delay_event` (TUM: OD or SBO — from blast exclusion zone or geotechnical exclusion) | Pillar 2 records; Pillar 6 governs | `risk_register_record`; mine controller's exclusion zone instruction | When a delay_event is created for a geotechnical exclusion zone or blast exclusion zone, it should carry a reference to the Pillar 6 `risk_register_record` entry that governs the exclusion. This enables the Contextualized analytics to decompose downtime by cause — including safety-driven stoppages — without conflating them with operational inefficiency delays. |
