# Pillar 5: Maintenance & Reliability

---

## Overview

Maintenance & Reliability is the operational integrity pillar — the processes that sustain the physical availability of all equipment and infrastructure required for Pillars 1-4 to function. It operates across all physical asset classes in the value chain: mobile mining equipment (Pillar 2), fixed processing plant equipment (Pillar 3), port and logistics infrastructure (Pillar 4), and the mine services equipment and geotechnical monitoring systems that underpin Pillar 2's Mine Services process area. While the original programme scope excluded Maintenance & Reliability as a first-class domain, the MFD data product's dependency on TUM: DT (Downtime) classifications and the broader programme's equipment availability analytics make it a required pillar.

The pillar operates across ISA-95 Levels 3 and 4 predominantly, with Level 2 involvement where condition monitoring sensors and SCADA alarm management feed predictive maintenance triggers, and Level 0-1 where physical inspection, testing, and repair activities occur. The governing standards are ISO 14224 (petroleum, petrochemical, and natural gas industries — collection and exchange of reliability and maintenance data for equipment), ISO 55000/55001 (asset management system requirements), and SAE JA1011/JA1012 (reliability-centred maintenance criteria). ISA-95 Part 3 (activity models for manufacturing operations management — maintenance management) provides the functional hierarchy for maintenance operations management.

The central data architecture challenge of this pillar is the bidirectional dependency with Pillar 2. Every TUM: DT (Downtime) event in Pillar 2 is a maintenance event in Pillar 5, and every maintenance work order in Pillar 5 is the resolution mechanism for a TUM: DT event in Pillar 2. The `work_order` entity in Pillar 5 and the `delay_event` entity in Pillar 2 must be explicitly linked — the delay event is the demand side, the work order is the supply side. Without that linkage, fleet availability analysis in the MFD Contextualized product cannot decompose downtime by failure mode, repair type, or maintenance strategy.

The pillar comprises four process areas: Maintenance Planning & Scheduling (translating asset health data and regulatory requirements into a forward maintenance plan), Preventive & Predictive Maintenance Execution (planned interventions that preserve equipment condition), Corrective/Reactive Maintenance (unplanned failure response), and Reliability Engineering & Asset Life Management (analysis, feedback, and strategy that closes the reliability improvement loop).

---

## Process Decomposition

> **Column guide**: ISA-95 Level 3 = operations management (CMMS/EAM, work order management, parts inventory). Level 4 = enterprise asset management (asset strategy, lifecycle planning, capital replacement). Levels 0-2 appear at condition monitoring sensor/SCADA interfaces and at physical inspection/repair activities. DDVC Layer: maintenance activities primarily generate Curated entities (work_order, equipment_failure_record, maintenance_task) that enable Contextualized products (equipment availability analytics, MTBF/MTTR, reliability trends).

## Process Flow Narratives (BPMN-Ready)

### 5.2 Preventive Maintenance — Process Flow (representative)

**Pool**: Preventive Maintenance | **Lanes**: FMS/EAM System, Maintenance Planner, Maintenance Supervisor, Tradesperson, Mine Controller

**Start Event**: PM trigger fires in EAM (hours threshold or calendar interval reached); work order released; parts kitted.

1. **Notify Equipment Operator and Mine Controller** (Maintenance Supervisor) — planned downtime window confirmed; equipment operator directed to park at maintenance bay.
2. **Take Equipment Out of Service** (Equipment Operator + Maintenance Supervisor) — equipment parked; FMS state changed to Maintenance; **TUM: DT begins** (timestamp recorded, linked to `work_order_id`); mine controller updates dispatch pool.
3. **Execute PM Tasks** (Tradesperson) — tasks executed in order; each task signed off in EAM as complete; findings recorded (defects or abnormal conditions).
   - *[GATEWAY — Defect found during service?]* -> Yes: assess severity. If safety: P1 corrective work order raised (Pillar 6 notified if safety-significant); if non-safety: deferred work order raised (P3/P4). No: continue.
4. **Function Test and Pre-Start Inspection** (Tradesperson + Equipment Operator) — equipment started and tested; operator completes pre-start check.
   - *[GATEWAY — Function test passed?]* -> Yes: proceed to return to service. No: fault diagnosis initiated (enters 5.3 Corrective flow).
5. **Return Equipment to Service** (Equipment Operator + FMS) — equipment operator logs equipment as available; FMS state changed to Available; **TUM: DT ends** (end timestamp linked to `work_order_id`).
6. **Close Work Order** (Maintenance Supervisor) — actual timestamps, task completions, findings, and parts consumed recorded in EAM; work order closed.

**End Event**: Work order closed in EAM; equipment in Available state in FMS; TUM: DT delay event closed; data available for reliability analysis.

### 5.3 Corrective Maintenance — Process Flow (representative)

**Pool**: Corrective Maintenance | **Lanes**: Equipment Operator, Mine Controller, Maintenance Supervisor, Tradesperson, Procurement (if emergency parts)

**Start Event**: Equipment failure detected in service (operator report or automated alarm).

1. **Report Failure and Initiate TUM: DT** (Operator -> Mine Controller) — mine controller records TUM: DT in FMS from failure timestamp; corrective work request raised in EAM.
2. **Assess and Dispatch Trades** (Maintenance Supervisor) — fault assessed from description; priority set (P1 if safety or Class A); trade team dispatched.
3. **Isolate Equipment and Obtain Safety Permit** (Tradesperson + Pillar 6 PTW) — LOTO applied; **Permit to Work obtained from Pillar 6** (6.2) before work commences on isolated equipment.
4. **Diagnose Fault** (Tradesperson) — systematic diagnosis; failed component identified; ISO 14224 failure mode code assigned; findings recorded in work order.
5. **Check Parts Availability** (Tradesperson + Warehouse) — required parts checked in ERP MM.
   - *[GATEWAY — Parts in stock?]* -> Yes: issue from warehouse; proceed to repair. No: emergency procurement initiated (5.3.3); downtime continues until parts arrive.
6. **Execute Repair** (Tradesperson) — component replaced; reassembly; parts consumed recorded in work order.
7. **Cancel Safety Permit and Return to Service** (Tradesperson + Pillar 6) — LOTO removed; permit cancelled in Pillar 6 PTW system; function test performed.
8. **Return Equipment to Available and Close Work Order** (Mine Controller + Maintenance Supervisor) — FMS state changed to Available; TUM: DT closed; work order closed with complete failure and repair data.

**End Event**: Equipment returned to Available fleet; TUM: DT closed and linked to closed work order; equipment_failure_record committed for reliability analysis.

## Completeness Verification

### Known Gaps and Forward Dependencies

| # | Gap | Resolution Path |
|---|-----|---|
| G1 | `work_order_id` linkage to `delay_event` in Pillar 2 — this is the single most critical cross-pillar data linkage in the programme. Currently the FMS (Minestar/Wenco) and the EAM (SAP PM) are separate systems with no automatic bidirectional key exchange. In practice, mine controllers often record TUM: DT without a work order reference. | BEAM workshop Pillar 5 session must produce a decision on how this linkage is enforced: (a) FMS prompts mine controller to enter work order number when classifying DT events; (b) EAM generates a notification to FMS when a work order is opened/closed; or (c) the MFD curated product performs a time-window join between delay_event and work_order (lowest data quality but most practical short-term). |
| G2 | Processing plant equipment (SAG mill, flotation cells, filters — Pillar 3) should be in scope for Pillar 5, but their maintenance events don't generate TUM: DT records in FMS — they generate DCS/SCADA alarms and PI Historian events. The `equipment_failure_record` entity structure is the same, but the source system is different. The scope boundary between Pillar 5 and PI Historian/DCS for processing plant maintenance needs to be resolved. | Confirm at BEAM workshop whether processing plant equipment is included in SAP PM (EAM) or managed separately in a standalone CMMS. |
| G3 | Statutory inspection compliance records — the link between regulatory inspection schedule and work order completion (proof of compliance) is referenced in 5.1.2.1 but not fully modelled. Regulatory non-compliance carries legal liability. The `maintenance_task` entity needs a `regulatory_requirement_ref` attribute and there must be a compliance tracking entity or report. | Flag for Pillar 6 (Health & Safety) — regulatory inspection compliance is a shared concern between Pillar 5 and Pillar 6. The compliance record should live in Pillar 6's data model and be linked to the Pillar 5 work order. |
| G4 | Shutdown and turnaround planning — major planned shutdowns (SAG mill liner change, flotation cell rebuild, crusher mantle replacement) require multi-week planning, significant resource mobilisation, and production curtailment. These are a distinct maintenance mode not decomposed to L4 here (they are captured at the work order level). | Flag for a future L3 decomposition of Shutdown & Turnaround as a sub-process of 5.1 and 5.2 — significant enough to warrant its own planning and execution model, but deferred for programme maturity reasons. |
