# Pillar 3: Processing & Beneficiation

---

## Overview

Processing & Beneficiation transforms Run-of-Mine (ROM) ore delivered from mine extraction into saleable concentrate product. The pillar spans six process areas: Comminution (size reduction), Concentration by flotation (selective mineral recovery), Dewatering & Product Handling (moisture reduction to specification), Metallurgical Accounting & Quality (mass balance closure and commercial metal accounting), Tailings Management (transport, classification, deposition, embankment construction and storage safety at the Tailings Storage Facility, TSF) and Water Management (makeup supply, reclaim, seepage recovery, process water distribution and the site water balance). The process boundary opens at ROM ore receipt at the primary crusher and closes at concentrate delivery to storage, tailings stored at the TSF, and the period water balance. Mine dewatering (Pillar 2) enters the water balance at the site boundary.

This pillar is governed by a continuous process paradigm: unlike the discrete, event-driven Load & Haul cycle, processing operates as a flowing system with feed entering, products and tailings exiting, and recirculating loads creating closed circuits within the plant. ISA-95 Levels 0–2 dominate — physical process, sensors, and SCADA/DCS control loops. Level 3 functions (metallurgical accounting, LIMS, quality management, shift reporting) are critical but consume data generated at Levels 0–2. Level 4 (production planning, plant utilisation targets) is a consumer rather than a generator within this pillar.

AMIRA P754 is the governing standard for every measurement point involving mass flow, grade, density, or moisture. Every process node in this pillar that generates a measurement is annotated with: measurement type, method, frequency, AMIRA principle(s), and custody transfer status. This annotation discipline is mandatory — it is the design input for the curated data product entities that will underpin metallurgical accounting and reconciliation. The mass balance node maps for each flotation process pattern are produced as a separate artifact within this pillar.

Process groups and L4 steps in this pillar are site- and mineral-agnostic. Ore types differ in configuration, not in process: flotation is modelled as two reusable patterns — the **Flotation Circuit** (3.2.2), run once per target mineral or bulk concentrate and chained through each circuit's tailings, and **Concentrate Separation** (3.2.3), which splits a bulk concentrate into two concentrates. A copper porphyry runs one circuit; a copper-molybdenum operation runs a bulk circuit then a separation; copper-zinc and lead-zinc operations run two circuits in sequence. Which process groups a site runs, how many instances, in what order, and with what reagent scheme and product specification is configuration data, confirmed per site in the BEAM overlay (see *Circuit Configuration Variants*). Mineral and site names in the rows are illustrations only.

### 3.2 Concentration (Flotation)

> **Process patterns.** Flotation is modelled as two reusable process groups. **3.2.2 Flotation Circuit** recovers one target mineral, or several together as a bulk concentrate, and runs once per configured circuit; circuits are chained by feeding the tailings of one circuit to the next. **3.2.3 Concentrate Separation** splits a bulk concentrate into two concentrates. ## Mass Balance Node Maps

Node maps are drawn per **process pattern**, not per commodity. A site's plant is a chain of pattern instances; each instance carries the same measurement-point roles. Measurement points are identified by role and instance, written `MP-05[Cu]` (role MP-05 in the Cu circuit) or `MP-09[Cu-Mo]` (role MP-09 in the Cu-Mo separation).

### Circuit Sequencing

The plant-level balance is the chain of instances. Each circuit's tailings are the next circuit's feed, so `MP-04[n] = MP-07[n+1]` for consecutive circuits, and the last circuit's MP-04 together with any other tailings streams forms MP-06. Typical chains are in *Circuit Configuration Variants*.

## Process Flow Narratives (BPMN-Ready)

### 3.1 Comminution — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Comminution | **Lanes**: Mine Interface / FMS, DCS / Auto-control, Control Room Operator, Metallurgist

**Start Event**: ROM ore available at crusher pocket (haul truck tip event or ROM stockpile reclaim active); plant in Run state.

1. **Receive ROM Ore at Crusher** (DCS + FMS interface) — tip event recorded; apron feeder belt weigher starts logging mass rate to PI Historian. **P754 MP-01 active — opening mass measurement**.
2. **Execute Primary Crushing** (DCS Auto-control) — crusher CSS maintained at setpoint; power draw and vibration monitored; tramp metal detection active.
3. **Classify Crusher Product at Discharge Conveyor** (DCS) — belt weigher on discharge conveyor logs secondary mass measurement (cross-check against MP-01). Product conveyed to COS.
   - *[Configuration: SABC circuit — proceed to SAG mill feed (3.1.3). Three-stage crushing circuit — proceed to secondary/tertiary crushing (3.1.2).]*
4. **Manage COS Level** (DCS Auto-control / Control Room Operator) — COS level sensor monitored; reclaim feeders controlled to maintain target SAG mill feed rate.
   - *[GATEWAY — COS level below minimum?]* → Yes: reduce SAG mill feed rate (DCS auto-throttle); alert to control room. No: continue.
5. **[CONDITIONAL — Secondary/Tertiary Crushing — three-stage crushing circuits]** — ore passes through secondary cone crusher → vibrating screen → tertiary cone crusher → screen undersize forward to ball mill feed.
6. **Operate SAG Mill** (DCS Auto-control) — mill speed, bearing pressure, water addition, and feed rate controlled by DCS; power draw optimised; MWD load estimate applied.
   - *[GATEWAY — SAG discharge screen generating excess pebbles?]* → Yes: pebble crusher activated (3.1.2.3 — SABC circuits). No: undersize direct to ball mill.
7. **Operate Ball Mill** (DCS Auto-control) — sump level controlled; cyclone feed pump speed varied to maintain sump.
8. **Classify via Hydrocyclone** (DCS Auto-control) — cyclone feed density and flow monitored; overflow reports to flotation feed sump; underflow recirculates.
   - *[GATEWAY — Cyclone overflow P80 off-target?]* → Yes: metallurgist adjusts cyclone apex, feed density, or mill throughput. No: continue.
9. **Add Grinding Media** (Plant Operator — scheduled) — balls added to SAG and/or ball mill per ball addition schedule.

**End Event**: Cyclone overflow at target P80 reporting continuously to flotation feed; all telemetry and mass measurement data streaming to PI Historian.

### 3.2 Concentration (Flotation) — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Flotation | **Lanes**: Plant Chemist / Metallurgist, DCS / Auto-control, Metallurgist

**Start Event**: Cyclone overflow feeding flotation feed sump; cells running.

**[NOTE — The narrative describes the two reusable patterns. A site runs one Flotation Circuit per configured target mineral or bulk concentrate — in sequence through tailings, or in parallel on split feeds — and a Concentrate Separation only where a bulk concentrate is floated. The active configuration may change by ore-type campaign. See *Circuit Configuration Variants*.]**

**L2 sequence:**
1. **3.2.1 Reagent Management & Pulp Conditioning** — condition the circuit feed for the circuit's target mineral.
2. **3.2.2 Flotation Circuit** — produces the circuit's final concentrate and its tailings, which leave in parallel:
   - Concentrate: *[GATEWAY — Concentrate destination?]* → Saleable (default): concentrate to dewatering. Bulk: **3.2.3 Concentrate Separation** → concentrates to dewatering. Waste: waste concentrate to tailings management (e.g., pyrite for low-sulphur tailings).
   - Tailings: *[GATEWAY — Another target mineral in tailings?]* → Yes: return to **3.2.1** to condition the tailings for the next circuit, then run **3.2.2** again for that circuit. No: final tailings to tailings management (3.3.3).

**Reagent management and conditioning (3.2.1):** start "Circuit feed or reagent replenishment due" → **3.2.1.1 Manage Reagent Inventory and Preparation** → **3.2.1.2 Adjust Pulp pH**.
   - *[GATEWAY — Activation or depression required?]* → Yes: **3.2.1.3 Activate or Depress Minerals for Target Circuit**, then continue. No: continue.
   - **3.2.1.4 Dose Collector and Frother** → circuit feed conditioned.

**Flotation circuit (3.2.2):**
1. **3.2.2.1 Operate Rougher Flotation** (DCS Auto-control + Metallurgist oversight) — OSA scans rougher concentrate and tail (MP-02, MP-03).
   - *[GATEWAY — Rougher tail grade above threshold?]* → Yes: **Raise collector dose or cut air rate; log change** (narrative-only). No: continue.
2. **3.2.2.2 Operate Scavenger Flotation** (DCS Auto-control) — tail grade monitored by OSA (MP-04).
   - *[GATEWAY — Scavenger tail grade above limit?]* → Yes: **Investigate feed, reagent or OSA fault; escalate** (narrative-only). No: continue.
3. **3.2.2.3 Operate Regrind Mill** (DCS Auto-control) — where installed.
4. **3.2.2.4 Operate Cleaner Flotation** (DCS Auto-control + Metallurgist) — final concentrate (MP-05); shift composite collected.
   - *[GATEWAY — Concentrate grade below specification?]* → Yes: **Adjust froth depth or cleaning; log change** (narrative-only). No: continue.

**End Event (3.2.2)**: Final concentrate and circuit tailings leave the circuit.

**Concentrate separation (3.2.3):** start "Bulk concentrate from flotation circuit" → **3.2.3.1 Prepare Bulk Concentrate for Separation** (MP-08) → **Condition pulp for separation (3.2.1)** (narrative-only; invokes the conditioning pattern) → **3.2.3.2 Operate Separation Rougher Flotation** → in parallel: sink concentrate to dewatering (MP-10); **3.2.3.3 Operate Separation Cleaner Flotation**.
   - *[GATEWAY — Float product below spec?]* → Yes: **Adjust depressant dose or cleaning; log change** (narrative-only). No: float concentrate to dewatering (MP-09).

### 3.3 Dewatering & Product Handling — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Dewatering & Product Handling | **Lanes**: DCS / Auto-control, Control Room Operator / Metallurgist, Plant Operator, Process Technician

**Start Event**: A saleable concentrate or the final tailings stream is available for thickening. The concentrate trains (one per saleable product) and the tailings duty run in parallel; each is an instance of the patterns below.

**L2 sequence:** **3.3.1 Thickening** → *[GATEWAY — Stream type?]* → Tailings: message end "Thickened tailings to tailings management (3.5)". Concentrate (default): **3.3.2 Concentrate Filtration** → *[GATEWAY — Drying or packaging required?]* → Yes: **3.3.3 Concentrate Drying & Packaging**. No: continue → concentrate products handled.

**Thickening (3.3.1)** — one instance per thickener duty (concentrate, bulk concentrate, tailings):
1. **3.3.1.1 Feed Slurry to Thickener** (DCS) — feed flowmeter and densitometer log mass rate continuously (MP-06 for the tailings duty).
2. **3.3.1.2 Dose Flocculant** (DCS + Metallurgist).
3. In parallel:
   - **3.3.1.4 Recover Thickener Overflow Water** (DCS) → recovered water to process water (WP-05).
   - *[GATEWAY — Underflow density below minimum?]* → Yes: **Reduce feed rate or increase flocculant** (narrative-only, Control Room Operator). No: continue. Then **3.3.1.3 Pump Thickener Underflow** (DCS) → underflow to its configured destination.

**Concentrate filtration (3.3.2)** — start "Underflow in filter feed tank":
1. **3.3.2.1 Operate Filter Press or Disc Filter** (DCS Auto-control).
2. In parallel:
   - **3.3.2.4 Recover Filtrate Water** (DCS) → recovered water to process water (WP-06).
   - **3.3.2.2 Discharge Filter Cake** (Plant Operator) → **3.3.2.3 Verify Moisture Content** (Process Technician).
     - *[GATEWAY — Moisture within specification?]* → Yes: **Transfer filter cake to concentrate shed** (narrative-only) → message end "Filter cake to shed (CTP-2)". No: **Adjust filter cycle, wash water or air; check underflow density** (narrative-only) and re-filter (loop to 3.3.2.1).

**Drying and packaging (3.3.3)** — start "Filter cake requires drying or packing":
   - *[GATEWAY — Drying specified?]* → Yes: **3.3.3.1 Dry Concentrate**. No: continue.
   - *[GATEWAY — Packed dispatch specified?]* → Yes: **3.3.3.2 Package and Weigh Concentrate** → message end "Packed units to Logistics (Pillar 4)". No: end "Dried concentrate to storage".

**End Event**: Concentrate products in storage or packed for dispatch; thickened tailings handed to 3.5; recovered water handed to 3.6.

### 3.4 Metallurgical Accounting & Quality — Process Flow Narrative

**Pool**: Metallurgical Accounting | **Lanes**: Auto-samplers / Instruments, Laboratory, Metallurgist, Chief Metallurgist, Systems

**Start Event**: Shift start — all auto-samplers active; PI Historian recording all P754 measurement points.

**[CONTINUOUS DURING SHIFT]**

1. **Collect Auto-Sampler Increments** (Auto-samplers) — increments collected at 15–60 min intervals across all designated streams; accumulated in shift composite cutters.
2. **Monitor OSA Data** (Metallurgist) — real-time grade trends reviewed; process adjustments made; OSA data archived to PI Historian continuously.
3. **Collect Grab/Composite Samples** (Process Technician) — as per shift sampling plan; submitted to LIMS.

**[AT SHIFT END]**

4. **Submit Shift Composite Samples to LIMS** (Process Technician) — shift composite samples registered in LIMS with source stream, collection interval, shift ID, and chain-of-custody record.
5. **Prepare Samples in Laboratory** (Lab Technician) — drying, pulverising, splitting; QC inserts (CRM, duplicate, blank) at defined rates.
6. **Analyse Samples** (Lab Analyst) — ICP/XRF analysis per assay method; results entered in LIMS.
7. **Review QA/QC** (Lab Analyst + Chief Metallurgist) — CRM recovery, duplicate RPD, blank contamination reviewed.
   - *[GATEWAY — QC batch accepted?]* → Yes: results released in LIMS. No: re-assay instruction issued; affected results flagged; batch held.
8. **Close Shift Mass Balance** (Metallurgist) — fetch mass flow data from PI Historian; fetch assay results from LIMS; calculate shift balance.
   - *[GATEWAY — Balance error within tolerance?]* → Yes: shift balance accepted and filed. No: investigation initiated (check instrument data, sample integrity, stockpile changes); escalate to Chief Metallurgist.
9. **Produce Daily Reconciliation** (Metallurgist) — aggregate shift balances; cross-check against independent measurements; report issued.

**[MONTHLY]**

10. **Close Monthly Metal Accounting Period** (Chief Metallurgist) — aggregate daily reconciliations; apply certification assay results; reconcile with outbound weighbridge and commercial CoA; produce formal monthly report; submit to Finance and Audit Committee.
11. **Conduct Stocktake** (Chief Metallurgist + Survey Team) — physical measurement of all in-process inventory; variance to book inventory calculated; adjustment entries made.

**End Event**: Monthly metal accounting report filed; P754-compliant documentation archived; Finance receives production figures for revenue recognition.

## Circuit Configuration Variants

Process groups and L4 steps in this pillar are site- and mineral-agnostic. Sites differ in **configuration**: which optional process groups run, how many instances of a pattern run, in what order, and with what reagent scheme and product specification. Configuration is data (`flotation_circuit`, `process_node`, `concentrate_type`) and is confirmed per site in the BEAM overlay. Site names below are indicative examples only and must be confirmed in each product's overlay.

### Flotation configurations by ore type

| Ore type | Flotation configuration (pattern instances in order) | Products | Example Teck sites (indicative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper porphyry | 3.2.2 [Cu] | Cu concentrate | QB, HVC, CdA (where Mo is not recovered) |
| Copper-molybdenum porphyry | 3.2.2 [bulk Cu-Mo] → 3.2.3 [Cu-Mo: Mo float, Cu sink] | Cu concentrate, Mo concentrate | QB, HVC |
| Copper-zinc (sequential) | 3.2.2 [Cu] → tailings → 3.2.2 [Zn] | Cu concentrate, Zn concentrate | Antamina |
| Copper-zinc-lead (sequential, Pb last) | 3.2.2 [Cu] → 3.2.2 [Zn] → 3.2.2 [Pb] | Cu, Zn, Pb concentrates | Antamina (where the Pb circuit is active) |
| Lead-zinc (differential, Pb first) | 3.2.2 [Pb] → tailings → 3.2.2 [Zn] | Pb concentrate, Zn concentrate | Red Dog |
| Copper-lead-zinc (sequential, Pb second) | 3.2.2 [Cu] → 3.2.2 [Pb] → 3.2.2 [Zn] | Cu, Pb, Zn concentrates | — |
| Copper-lead-zinc (bulk, then separate) | 3.2.2 [bulk Cu-Pb] → 3.2.3 [Cu-Pb] ; bulk circuit tailings → 3.2.2 [Zn] | Cu, Pb, Zn concentrates | — |
| Copper-zinc with molybdenum | 3.2.2 [bulk Cu-Mo] → 3.2.3 [Cu-Mo] ; bulk circuit tailings → 3.2.2 [Zn] | Cu, Mo, Zn concentrates | — |
| Any ore type, with sulphide removal from tailings | … → last circuit tailings → 3.2.2 [pyrite]; pyrite concentrate as waste to separate deposition | Products as above; low-sulphur tailings; sulphide concentrate (waste) | — |

Precious metals (Au, Ag) normally report to one of these concentrates as payable credits and need no separate flotation group. Recovery by gravity, leaching or solvent extraction and electrowinning (SX-EW) uses different process groups and is out of scope for this pillar.

Circuit instances can also run **in parallel** on split feeds (e.g., a coarse-particle circuit on a cyclone underflow split alongside a conventional circuit), each with its own `feed_source`, and a site may **campaign** ore types, switching the active configuration (`operating_mode`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`).

### Other configuration variants

| Process Area | Variation | Configuration options | Data Implication |
|--------------|-----------|-----------------------|-----------------|
| **3.1.2 Crushing** | Crushing circuit | SABC (SAG + ball mill + pebble crushing: 3.1.2.3 applies) or three-stage crushing before ball milling (3.1.2.1–3.1.2.2 apply; no SAG mill) | `process_node` holds the crusher and screen nodes a site has; the SAG mill node is absent where there is three-stage crushing |
| **3.1.3 Grinding** | Mill types | SAG + ball mill, or ball mill only | Mill telemetry entity must allow for a missing SAG mill |
| **3.2.1 Conditioning** | Reagent scheme per circuit | pH setpoint, activator (e.g., CuSO₄ for sphalerite), depressants (e.g., NaCN, ZnSO₄, NaHS, sodium silicate), collector and frother | `flotation_circuit` references its reagent scheme; `reagent_addition_record` is per addition point, so per circuit |
| **3.2.2 Flotation Circuit** | Circuit design | Cleaner stages; cleaner-scavenger instead of rougher-scavenger; regrind installed or not; column cells | Circuit design is `process_node` configuration, not a different process |
| **3.3 Dewatering** | Thickener duties, trains and location | Thickening (3.3.1) per saleable concentrate, bulk concentrate and tailings; one filtration train per saleable concentrate, at the concentrator or remote (e.g., at a port after a slurry pipeline — Pillar 4 transport); drying and packaging (3.3.3) where the product requires it | `concentrate_type` carries the moisture specification and packaging basis; thickener and filter nodes link to their duty; remote filtration has its own `water_system` |
| **3.3.2 Filtration** | Product moisture spec | Commodity- and contract-dependent (typically Cu 8–12%, Zn 8–10%, Pb 8–10%); cold-climate long storage makes moisture critical | Moisture QC compares against the `concentrate_type` specification |
| **3.4 Metal Accounting** | Metals in scope | The payable and penalty elements of every configured concentrate (e.g., Cu, Mo, Zn, Pb, Ag, Au, Cd) | Balance and production records must hold any metal by key, not a fixed set of metal columns |
| **3.5 Tailings** | Facility design | Deposition method (conventional slurry, thickened, paste); construction method (upstream, centreline, downstream); construction materials (classified sand, mine waste, rockfill, borrow); classification fitted or not | `tailings_facility` holds the configuration; `facility_location` holds cells, paddocks, zones and ponds |
| **3.5 Tailings** | Facility count and routing | One or more facilities; discharge lines, emergency discharge and classification stations | Routes are `process_node` configuration; deposition records carry location |
| **3.6 Water** | Makeup source and treatment | Seawater (desalination), surface water, groundwater or third-party supply; treatment and residue disposal where required; shared supply systems | `water_system.source_type`, `treatment_type`, `shared_with`; allocation rules applied in 3.6.5.2 |
| **3.6 Water** | Recovery routes | Reclaim users (process water, classification station); seepage destinations (impoundment, reclaim, treatment and discharge); remote water systems (e.g., port filtrate) | Each route is a `water_transfer_record` between `water_system` records, with an internal-recycle flag |

Belt weighers, density gauges, flowmeters, and analytical instruments must all have current calibration certificates. Instrument calibration work orders in Pillar 5 must reference the P754 measurement point ID (from the `amira_measurement_point` entity in Pillar 3) — this enables the metallurgical accountant to confirm that all measurement points were within calibration at the time of each mass balance closure. |

### Pillar 6 (Health & Safety) Dependencies

| Pillar 3 Entity / Activity | Dependency Direction | Pillar 6 Entity / Activity | Integration Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.2.1.1 Manage Reagent Inventory and Preparation | Pillar 6 governs | `risk_register_record` (chemical hazards: xanthate, NaCN, lime, CuSO4); `exposure_monitoring_record` (airborne NaCN, lime dust) | Flotation reagents include potentially hazardous chemicals: sodium cyanide (NaCN — acute toxic, asphyxiation risk if reacts with acid), lime dust (respiratory hazard), MIBC frother (flammable), xanthates (flammable, spontaneous ignition risk). Pillar 5 maintenance work orders for XRF analyzer maintenance must trigger Pillar 6 radiation work permits. |
