# Pillar 6: Health & Safety

---

## Overview

Health & Safety is the operational licence pillar — it does not produce product, but no product is produced without it. Every other pillar in the value chain operates within a safety framework that Pillar 6 defines, governs, and enforces. The distinction from Pillars 1-5 is architectural: where those pillars describe what work is done, Pillar 6 describes the conditions under which work is permitted to happen. Its primary data outputs are risk controls, permits, and incident records — not physical product or commercial documents.

The pillar is structured around five process areas: Hazard Identification & Risk Control (the systematic identification and mitigation of workplace hazards), Permit to Work (the formal control of high-risk activities through a documented authorisation system), Incident Management (the detection, investigation, and corrective action system for safety events), Occupational Health Monitoring (the surveillance of worker health exposures and outcomes), and Safety Performance & Regulatory Compliance (reporting, audit, and regulatory obligation management). These five areas collectively generate the safety governance data layer that crosscuts all other pillars.

The governing standards are ISO 45001:2018 (Occupational Health and Safety Management Systems — Requirements), the ICMM Health & Safety Framework (International Council on Mining and Metals — the primary voluntary industry framework applied at Teck), and site-applicable regulation: MSHA (Mine Safety and Health Administration) at Red Dog, WorkSafeBC at HVC, SERNAGEOMIN at QB and CdA, and Peru's Ministry of Energy and Mines (MINEM) at Antamina. Regulatory divergence across jurisdictions is the most material commodity/site variation in this pillar — the underlying process structure is identical, but the specific regulatory obligations, reporting forms, and inspection schedules differ significantly by jurisdiction.

The Permit to Work (PTW) system is the most data-critical process area for cross-pillar integration. Every maintenance activity involving energy isolation (Pillar 5, 5.2 and 5.3), every blast exclusion zone (Pillar 2, 2.1.4.1), every confined space entry, and every work-at-height activity requires a formal permit. The `permit_to_work` entity is a gate record — it must be open and in force before the associated Pillar 5 work order or Pillar 2 exclusion zone can proceed, and it must be formally cancelled on completion. This bidirectional dependency between PTW records and operational activity records is the critical integration point for the programme.

## Process Decomposition

> **Column guide**: ISA-95 Level 3 = operations management (safety management system, permit system, incident management). Level 4 = enterprise safety governance (regulatory reporting, audit, policy). Levels 0-1 appear at physical monitoring (gas detection, noise dosimetry, atmospheric sampling). DDVC Layer: safety activities primarily generate Curated entities (permit_to_work, incident_record, risk_control_record, health_monitoring_record) that are referenced by activities in Pillars 1-5 as gate conditions, and consumed by Contextualized products (safety performance analytics, regulatory compliance reporting).

## Interaction & Data Flow Map

### Cross-Pillar Interactions — Pillar 6

| Interaction | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Pillar 6 -> All Pillars (gate function)** | Pillar 6 is the safety gate for all operational activities. The `permit_to_work` entity must be Active before any Pillar 5 maintenance work involving energy isolation commences (5.2.1.1, 5.3.2.1). Blast exclusion zone enforcement (Pillar 2, 2.1.4.1) is a parallel Pillar 6 control — the exclusion zone is an operational safety control equivalent to a permit. FLHA records (6.1.2) are a prerequisite for every task in every pillar. |
| **Pillar 6 -> Pillar 5 (maintenance gate)** | `permit_to_work` must be issued and Active before Pillar 5 work on isolated equipment commences. `permit_to_work` cancellation is a gate condition for Pillar 5 work order closure (5.2.1.3, 5.3.2.3). Safety defect reports from pre-use checks generate P1 work orders in Pillar 5. Maintenance incidents (injuries during maintenance) generate incident records in Pillar 6 linked to the associated work order in Pillar 5. |
| **Pillar 6 -> Pillar 2 (blast and operations)** | Pre-blast safety clearance and exclusion zone enforcement (2.1.4.1 and 2.1.4.3) are Pillar 6 controls applied within the Pillar 2 process. Slope monitoring alerts (Pillar 2, 2.3.3) that trigger exclusion zones are safety controls governed by Pillar 6 geotechnical risk assessment. TUM: DT events caused by safety-related stoppages (including blast exclusion zones) should carry a Pillar 6 `hazard_category_id` reference. |
| **Pillar 6 -> Pillar 1 (planning constraint)** | Geotechnical exclusion zones (Pillar 2, 2.3.3 — governed by Pillar 6 risk assessment) constrain the short-term mining plan. Regulatory inspection schedules and statutory inspection requirements (Pillar 5 work orders for statutory inspections, governed by Pillar 6 `regulatory_requirement` entity) are constraints on fleet availability. |
| **Pillar 6 -> Pillar 3 (processing safety)** | Confined space entry permits for process plant equipment (thickeners, tanks, vessels) are Pillar 6 PTWs for Pillar 3 maintenance activities. Reagent-related exposure monitoring (Pillar 6, 6.4) governs chemical handling practices in the reagent management process (Pillar 3, 3.2.1). |
| **Incident investigation data dependencies** | `incident_record` investigation consumes operational data from across all pillars: FMS equipment state history (Pillar 2 — for vehicle interaction incidents), PI Historian process data (Pillar 3 — for process plant incidents), PTW records (Pillar 6 — for maintenance incidents), and grade control and blast records (Pillar 2 — for drill & blast incidents). This makes PI Historian and the safety management system the two most cross-cutting data assets in the programme. |

## Completeness Verification

### Known Gaps and Forward Dependencies

| # | Gap | Resolution Path |
|---|-----|---|
| G1 | PTW system technology is not specified — many mine sites still use paper-based PTW systems. A paper PTW generates no digital record unless it is retrospectively entered into the safety management system. The `permit_to_work` entity design assumes digital PTW — the data product contract must specify whether Teck sites use digital or paper PTW, and the data capture pathway for paper permits. | Confirm PTW system type at each site as part of BEAM workshop Pillar 6 data audit. |
| G2 | The linkage between `incident_record` and operational pillar data (FMS, PI Historian, PTW records) requires the incident investigation team to have read access to systems from Pillars 2, 3, 5, and 6. Current access controls across these systems may not support cross-system investigation data pulls. | Flag as a data governance and access control dependency — the `incident_record` entity must carry foreign key references to all relevant operational records, which requires agreement on cross-system data sharing for investigation purposes. |
| G3 | Contractor safety management — a significant proportion of mining workforce (drill & blast contractors, maintenance contractors, transport carriers) are not direct Teck employees. Contractor safety data (incidents, PTW compliance, exposure monitoring) must be captured in the same system as direct employee data for the LTIFR/TRIFR denominators to be accurate. | Confirm contractor data inclusion policy with the corporate HSE team before designing the `incident_record` and `exposure_monitoring_record` entities. |
| G4 | The ICMM fatal hazard controls framework identifies 13 categories. Teck's programme may use a subset or a customised version. The `hazard_category` reference entity must be aligned to Teck's specific HSE taxonomy, not just the ICMM default. | BEAM workshop Pillar 6 session must validate the `hazard_category` reference data against Teck's internal HSE taxonomy. |
| G5 | Red Dog MSHA regulatory complexity — MSHA has significantly more prescriptive data and record-keeping requirements than provincial or national regulators at other Teck sites (e.g., required retention of training records, exposure monitoring records, and PTW records for defined periods). The `regulatory_compliance_record` entity must accommodate MSHA-specific record types that do not exist at other sites. | Flag Red Dog MSHA compliance data requirements as a site-specific extension to the `regulatory_compliance_record` entity — to be confirmed during BEAM workshop Pillar 6 with Red Dog safety team. |
